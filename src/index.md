## "The internet bubble popped, but we still use the internet so AI bubble will be the same"

This is a false equivalence fallacy used by many AI bros around the internet. AI leans on very different technology with very different properties and computing requirements than the early internet.

### Why the internet survived

When the dotcom bubble popped, many companies went bankrupt, but the internet stayed for a very simple reason: **the internet works on lightweight and open technologies and protocols**.

The internet is a distributed system and doesn't need any particular company to exist, it doesn't need data centers as power hungry as AI data centers with run on power hungry Nvidia chips that cost as much as a used car per unit. You can easily make your own http server in [C](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2HrYIl6GpYg) or [even in your own custom language](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oE6_Ue0ZTHg) without any libraries in a few hundred lines of code and you can host it on any device with an internet connection and anyone can just access it.

### The main problem: AI models are largely proprietary

AI companies have already pretty much scraped all the data from the internet there is to scrape to the point they've become so desperate they started doing destructive scanning on rare books ([Anthropic cof cof](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2026/aug/05/anthropic-ai-destroying-books)) and only they have the files containing the weights of the models and then you pay for *(1)* the license to have access to the model and *(2)* running the models on their servers.

This essentially means that if they were to die tomorrow we would lose those frontier AI models forever, and making new LLMs is also not easy and no one has that kind of money and infrastructure and most hardware still can't run LLMs large enough to do anything useful or fast enough. On the average computer with integrated GPUs it will run at less than 1 token per second and will take multiple minutes just to load the model. And even if you have the hardware they still suck compared to the frontier models.

And this is why if the AI bubble pops and the AI companies die, LLMs as a technology won't disappear (they already existed before ChatGPT), but **don't expect to have access to the kind of frontier models we have today or that they will be economically viable or somehow more affordable than today**. The current trend is actually more like the opposite, AI is getting more expensive as new models come out and companies run out of money and [change their business models to more expensive ones to try to compensate for their debt](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001625-understanding-chatgpt-and-codex-usage-limits).
